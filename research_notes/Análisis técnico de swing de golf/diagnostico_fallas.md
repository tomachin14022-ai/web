# Diagnosing golf swing faults from 2D video (down-the-line and face-on): definitions, video criteria, consequences, and physical causes

Evidence tags used throughout:
- **[TPI]**: TPI's own wording or claims (proprietary; mostly no published method).
- **[Research]**: peer-reviewed study.
- **[Vendor data]**: measurements published by a technology or teaching company (GOLFTEC, Sportsbox), not peer-reviewed.
- **[Instructor]**: instructor opinion or teaching convention.
- **[Low quality]**: unattributed blog or forum. Not to be used as a criterion.

Abbreviations: DTL = down-the-line (camera behind the golfer, looking down the target line). FO = face-on (camera in front of the golfer). RH = right-handed.

---

## Q1. How does TPI define and identify each of its 16 swing characteristics, and which screen tests correlate with each?

### Takeaway
TPI publishes one page per characteristic on mytpi.com. Each page gives a definition and, for a few characteristics, an explicit video method:
- **Over the Top:** DTL "slot" lines.
- **Flat Shoulder Plane:** DTL spine line plus a parallel line above the trail shoulder.
- **Flying Elbow:** DTL, elbow pointing behind the golfer at the top.
- **Late Buckle:** visible from both views.

Most pages give **no numeric thresholds**. The only numbers are TPI's "normal" lateral shifts, about 4 in for the pelvis and 2–3 in for the upper body. The pages link each characteristic to physical limitations and screen tests in prose. The percentage odds tying screen failures to characteristics are TPI claims passed along by TPI-certified trainers. Only one peer-reviewed study (Gulgin et al. 2014) tested these links, and it found only a few significant associations.

### Cited Findings

**TPI's framing.**
- TPI calls these "swing characteristics" rather than faults because a characteristic "doesn't preclude players from playing great golf." Some golfers early extend because of a technical inefficiency, and others because it's the most effective way to swing with their physical tools. [TPI] — [TPI article: Early Extension swing characteristic](https://www.mytpi.com/articles/swing/early-extension-swing-characteristic)
- The TPI index page describes the 16 as "the most common swing characteristics that can lead to mechanical inefficiencies in your golf swing, and put you at greater risk for injury." [TPI] — [TPI Swing Characteristics index](https://www.mytpi.com/improve-my-game/swing-characteristics)

#### 1. S-Posture (setup, P1)
- **Definition:** "a postural characteristic that can be caused by the player creating too much arch in their lower back by sticking their tail bone out to much in the setup position… This deactivation of the core muscles can cause a loss of posture or reverse spine angle during the backswing." [TPI] — [TPI S-Posture](https://www.mytpi.com/improve-my-game/swing-characteristics/s-posture)
- **Causes listed by TPI:**
  - "Lower Crossed Syndrome - tightness in the hip flexors and lower back and weakness in the abdominals and glutes";
  - not understanding how to bend from the hips;
  - being told to "stick their butt out";
  - lack of abdominal strength;
  - "Too much flex in the knees with the torso to upright."

  [TPI] — [TPI S-Posture](https://www.mytpi.com/improve-my-game/swing-characteristics/s-posture)
- Titleist says "S Posture is one of the most reliable predictors for low back pain." [TPI] — [Titleist: Three Postures in Golf](https://www.titleist.com/instruction/three-postures-in-golf)
- TPI claim relayed by a TPI-certified gym: "if you have an S-Posture setup then you have an 80% chance of early extension as well as an 80% chance of loss of posture." [TPI, second-hand] — [Pinnacle Fitness: TPI Physical Screen](https://pinnaclefitness.squarespace.com/blog/the-power-of-golf-tpi-screen)
- **View:** The S-Posture page gives no video method. DTL at P1 is the natural view for lumbar curvature (see Inferences).

#### 2. C-Posture (setup, P1)
- **Definition:** "a posture that occurs when your shoulders are slumped forward at address and you have a definitive roundedness to your thoracic spine." [TPI] — [TPI C-Posture](https://www.mytpi.com/improve-my-game/swing-characteristics/c-posture)
- **Causes:**
  - "Limited thoracic spine extension";
  - "Upper Crossed Syndrome" (tight pecs, lats, upper traps and levator scapulae; weak mid-scapular muscles, serratus anterior, lower traps and deep neck flexors);
  - scapular instability;
  - core instability;
  - lack of instruction;
  - "Lack of pelvic tilt causing the upper body to bend to address the ball";
  - "Clubs that are too short";
  - "Grip that is too much in the fingers of both hands."

  [TPI] — [TPI C-Posture](https://www.mytpi.com/improve-my-game/swing-characteristics/c-posture)
- TPI claim (second-hand): "if you have a C-Posture setup then you have a 60% chance of early extension and a 55% chance of having a flat shoulder plane." [TPI, second-hand] — [Pinnacle Fitness](https://pinnaclefitness.squarespace.com/blog/the-power-of-golf-tpi-screen)

#### 3. Loss of Posture (backswing through downswing)
- **Definition:** "any significant alteration from your body's original set up angles during your golf swing… Losing your spine angle or altering your posture usually causes two typical miss hits, the block to the right and a hook to the left." [TPI] — [TPI Loss of Posture](https://www.mytpi.com/improve-my-game/swing-characteristics/loss-of-posture)
- **Physical parameters and tests named by TPI:**
  - "any limitation in performing a full deep squat or full hip bend can force a player to lose their pelvic posture… best evaluated using the Overhead Deep Squat Test and the Toe Touch Test";
  - trunk–pelvis separation: "Seated Trunk Rotation Test, the Reach, Roll, and Lift Test, and the Lat Test";
  - core: "Pelvic Tilt Test and the Bridge w/ Leg Extension Test";
  - hip and shoulder flexibility: "90/90 Test, the Lat Test and the Lower Quarter Rotation Test."

  [TPI] — [TPI Loss of Posture](https://www.mytpi.com/improve-my-game/swing-characteristics/loss-of-posture)
- **View and threshold:** TPI gives no video method or numeric threshold on this page.

#### 4. Flat Shoulder Plane (backswing, P1 → P4)
- **Definition:** "In the ideal world the shoulders should move perpendicular to the tilt of the spine on the backswing. A flat shoulder plane is when the shoulders turn on a more horizontal plane than the axis of the original spine angle." [TPI] — [TPI Flat Shoulder Plane](https://www.mytpi.com/improve-my-game/swing-characteristics/flat-shoulder-plane)
- **Video method, DTL (verbatim):** "With the camera and the student swing in the setup position simply draw a line from the base of the tailbone to the back of the neck. Then draw another line just above the trailing shoulder and parallel to the spine angle line. Take the student to the top of the backswing and note if the lead shoulder is above the shoulder plane line. If the lead shoulder is above the line they have a flat shoulder plane." [TPI] — [TPI Flat Shoulder Plane](https://www.mytpi.com/improve-my-game/swing-characteristics/flat-shoulder-plane)
- **Consequences:** "creates a shift in swing planes on the downswing… compensations on the downswing with the body or hands to square the club face… loss of power… inconsistent ball striking." [TPI] — same page.
- **Tests named:** Seated Trunk Rotation, Reach Roll and Lift, Lat (Shoulder Flexion), Pelvic Tilt, Bridge w/ Leg Extension, 90/90, Lower Quarter Rotation. [TPI] — same page.
- The TPI index page describes it as "a standing up in the backswing and a flattening of the shoulder plane." [TPI] — [TPI index](https://www.mytpi.com/improve-my-game/swing-characteristics)
- **Titleist self-check:** hold a club across the back at setup. If at the top the shaft "points level with the horizon," the shoulder plane is too flat. [TPI/Titleist] — [Titleist: Flat Shoulder Plane](https://titleist.ca/videos/instruction/flat-shoulder-plane)

#### 5. Flying Elbow (P4)
- **Definition:** "the trailing elbow leaving the trailing side on the backswing. **It is not necessarily a fault and many great players play golf with this motion.** … The trailing elbow will fly away from the trailing side as the club reaches the top of the backswing, with the elbow pointing well behind the student. On most golfers the elbow points to the ground at the top of the backswing." [TPI] — [TPI Flying Elbow](https://www.mytpi.com/improve-my-game/swing-characteristics/flying-elbow)
- **Video method:** "You can diagnose the flying elbow from the target line view by watching to see if the trailing elbow is pointing behind the player at the top of the backswing position." [TPI] — same page.
- **Physical causes:** shoulder-girdle mobility restrictions in the trail shoulder, and thoracic spine limitation "coupled with loss of scapular stability… will limit external rotation and cause the elbow to fly." [TPI] — same page.
- **Related screen:** the 90/90 test measures shoulder external rotation; "limited external rotation could cause your shoulder to elevate or flare during the swing which will change the swing path." Pass: "forearm rotated past the spine angle." [TPI, second-hand] — [Pinnacle: 90/90](https://pinnaclefitness.squarespace.com/blog/the-power-of-golf-tpi-screen-9090)

#### 6. Early Extension (downswing, P5 → P7)
- **Definitions (TPI wording varies between pages):**
  - Characteristic page: "any forward movement (thrust) of the lower body towards the golf ball during the downswing." [TPI] — [TPI Early Extension](https://www.mytpi.com/improve-my-game/swing-characteristics/early-extension)
  - Article: "any forward movement of the pelvis toward the ball on the backswing or downswing." [TPI] — [TPI article](https://www.mytpi.com/articles/swing/early-extension-swing-characteristic)
  - Index page: "any movement towards the golf ball during the swing." [TPI] — [TPI index](https://www.mytpi.com/improve-my-game/swing-characteristics)
- **Consequences:** "causes the arms and club to get stuck behind your body during the downswing, and forces your torso to raise up and elevate through the hitting zone… the block to the right and a hook to the left… players… complain of getting stuck or trapped." [TPI] — [TPI Early Extension](https://www.mytpi.com/improve-my-game/swing-characteristics/early-extension). The TPI article adds fat and thin shots in the short game and "the dreaded shank." [TPI] — [TPI article](https://www.mytpi.com/articles/swing/early-extension-swing-characteristic)
- **Physical parameters:**
  - "any limitation in performing a full deep squat or full hip bend can force a player to early extend";
  - "If the pelvis is unable to rotate around the lead hip due to joint or muscular restrictions then forward and lateral movements will dominate";
  - limited trunk–pelvis separation ("reduced spinal and rib cage mobility and shortened lat flexibility");
  - gluteal and abdominal strength and control of the pelvis.

  [TPI] — [TPI Early Extension](https://www.mytpi.com/improve-my-game/swing-characteristics/early-extension)
- The TPI article singles out the Overhead Deep Squat: the inability to deep squat is "one of the most prevalent issues" in early extenders. [TPI] — [TPI article](https://www.mytpi.com/articles/swing/early-extension-swing-characteristic)
- **Prevalence:** TPI says it has screened over 90,000 golfers and "67% have early extension." [TPI, promotional; figure reported via search excerpt, verify on page] — [TPI article](https://www.mytpi.com/articles/swing/why-early-extension-causes-a-reduction-of-power-in-the-golf-swing)
- **Video method:** The TPI characteristic page gives none (no "tush line" text on the page). See Q3 for the measurement methods used by others.

#### 7. Over the Top (transition, P4 → P6)
- **Definition:** "occurs due to an overuse of the upper body on the downswing. As a result the club will be thrown on the outside of the intended swing plane with the club head approaching the ball from outside to in. This creates a pull if the clubface is square or a slice if the club face is open." [TPI] — [TPI Over the Top](https://www.mytpi.com/improve-my-game/swing-characteristics/over-the-top)
- **Video method, DTL (verbatim):** "pause the students swing at the address position, now using your graphic tools draw a line up the club shaft plane extending up past the shoulder of the player. Run the video until the lead forearm is just past parallel to the ground and draw a second line the length of the club from the grip end to the clubhead. You should have drawn what we term as the slot. Now run the video and as the player starts their downswing, does the club pass in between the slot or does the club come outside or above the slot. If the club is above the slot they have the Over the Top swing fault." [TPI] — same page.
- **Physical parameters:** weight shift from the back foot to the front foot initiated by the lower body. This "requires several physical factors including good hip mobility, a strong core and lower body (glutes and abs) and the ability to disassociate the lower body from the upper body." [TPI] — same page.

#### 8. Sway (backswing, P1 → P4)
- **Definition:** "any excessive lower body lateral movement away from the target during your backswing that forces your weight to the outside of your trail side foot." [TPI] — [TPI Sway](https://www.mytpi.com/improve-my-game/swing-characteristics/sway)
- **Physical parameters:**
  - "right hip internal rotation is paramount… If the body is unable to rotate around the right hip… lateral movements will dominate";
  - trunk–pelvis separation;
  - "the glute medius is the king."

  [TPI] — same page.
- **Video method:** The TPI page gives no line. V1 Golf uses "The right hip moving laterally past the right foot in the backswing. Best seen from face-on," with key frames P2–P4. [Instructor/vendor] — [V1: Hip Sway Analysis](https://swing-analysis.v1sports.com/swing-analysis/hip-sway-analysis)

#### 9. Slide (downswing, P4 → P7)
- **Definition:** "any excessive lower body lateral movement towards the target during your downswing." [TPI] — [TPI Slide](https://www.mytpi.com/improve-my-game/swing-characteristics/slide)
- **Physical parameters:** "lead hip internal rotation is paramount for full rotation into the lead hip"; trunk–pelvis separation; glute medius of the lead leg. [TPI] — same page.

#### 10. Late Buckle (just after impact, P7 → P8)
- **Definition:** "a swing characteristic that is seen immediately after impact… A Late Buckle is when a player actually dips down and drops after making contact with the ball. There is usually a sudden bend in the knees (or buckle of the lower body) that is clearly visible from both sides of the golfer." [TPI] — [TPI Late Buckle](https://www.mytpi.com/improve-my-game/swing-characteristics/late-buckle)
- **Causes:** "almost always" physical: "mobility restrictions in the lead hip, core weaknesses, lower back dysfunction or ankle restrictions… the knees will act like shock absorbers." [TPI] — same page.
- **Conflict:** the TPI index page calls it "almost the same as a slide but it happens after impact," while the detail page describes a vertical knee buckle and dip. [TPI] — [TPI index](https://www.mytpi.com/improve-my-game/swing-characteristics)

#### 11. Reverse Spine Angle (backswing, P4)
- **Definition:** "any excessive upper body backward bend (trunk leaning towards the target) or excessive left lateral upper body bend (for a right-handed player) during the backswing… one of the prime causes of lower back pain in golfers." [TPI] — [TPI Reverse Spine Angle](https://www.mytpi.com/improve-my-game/swing-characteristics/reverse-spine-angle)
- **Causes:**
  - limited trunk–pelvis separation;
  - trail-hip internal rotation ("Any lateral sway during the backswing will force the spine to tilt into backward bend");
  - core stability;
  - "too much pelvic tilt at address" (S-Posture).

  [TPI] — same page.
- V1 describes the related reverse pivot as "the spine tilting toward the target in the backswing… the head often moves left," checked at P4. [Instructor/vendor] — [V1: Reverse Pivot](https://swing-analysis.v1sports.com/swing-analysis/reverse-pivot-analysis)

#### 12. Forward Lunge (transition and downswing)
- **Definition with TPI's numbers:** "any excessive lateral move of the upper body towards the target during transition and the downswing. Normally, there is approximately a four inch lateral weight shift of the lower body towards the target in the downswing. The upper body tends to follow the lower body with about two to three inches of movement as well. In a forward lunge… the upper body having more movement than the lower body. This can create a very steep downward attack angle… very high shots with a lot of spin." [TPI] — [TPI Forward Lunge](https://www.mytpi.com/improve-my-game/swing-characteristics/forward-lunge)
- **Causes:** "hip mobility, glute and core weakness, ankle mobility limitations and lower body strength or power deficits." [TPI] — same page.

#### 13. Hanging Back (downswing to impact)
- **Definition:** "a lack of weight shift towards the target on the downswing. Usually the player is still on their back foot at impact. In a normal weight shift, the player is about four inches closer to the target at impact compared to address. Many times the player will hit heavy divots that begin behind the ball or… a ball position that is too far back." [TPI] — [TPI Hanging Back](https://www.mytpi.com/improve-my-game/swing-characteristics/hanging-back)
- **Causes:**
  - inability to post into the lead leg (lead hip and ankle mobility, core and lead-leg stability);
  - reverse pivot ("too much weight is put on the lead leg during the backswing, and then there is a corresponding weight shift to the back leg on the downswing");
  - lack of lower-body power.

  [TPI] — same page.

#### 14. Casting (downswing, P5 → P6)
- **Definition:** "an early release of the golf club during the downswing… a weak impact position with the left wrist being cupped at impact. It adds loft… At impact we should see the shaft leaning slightly toward the target." [TPI] — [TPI Casting](https://www.mytpi.com/improve-my-game/swing-characteristics/casting)
- **Causes:**
  - limited lower-body contribution (hip mobility, ankle restrictions, separation, glutes and abs);
  - wrist limitations: "Without extension in the trail wrist, it is difficult to maintain a good set during the downswing."

  [TPI] — same page.

#### 15. Scooping (through impact, P6 → P8)
- **Definition:** "a premature release of the wrist angles… left wrist being cupped at impact… usually combined with Casting or Early Release. It is termed Scooping when the club head passes the hands through impact and the student is trying to lift the ball into the air." [TPI] — [TPI Scooping](https://www.mytpi.com/improve-my-game/swing-characteristics/scooping)
- **Causes:** lower-body power, hip mobility and core strength; wrists: "The lead wrist should have good flexion and the trail wrist needs to be able to extend." [TPI] — same page.

#### 16. Chicken Winging (through impact, P7 → P8)
- **Definition:** "the appearance of the lead arm on players who bend their lead elbow and cup their lead wrist through impact… Normally, the lead arm is extended through impact to create as much width as possible… loss in power… add loft… excessive spin." [TPI] — [TPI Chicken Winging](https://www.mytpi.com/improve-my-game/swing-characteristics/chicken-winging)
- **Causes:**
  - lower-body power deficits;
  - "when the lower body does not initiate the sequence on the downswing, the swing path can become steep or Over-the-Top. Any steepening of the swing plane… can also lead to Chicken Winging";
  - "Loss of external rotation in the lead shoulder."

  [TPI] — same page.
- **V1 key frames:** the lead elbow "pointed down or flaring out" just after impact. [Instructor/vendor] — [V1: Chicken Wing](https://swing-analysis.v1sports.com/swing-analysis/chicken-wing-analysis)

#### Peer-reviewed evidence on screens and characteristics
- **Gulgin, Schulte & Crawley (2014), J Strength Cond Res 28(2):534–539.**
  - Method: TPI Level 1 screen versus 14 swing faults "as defined by the TPI," coded with V1 Pro from four 5-iron shots per golfer.
  - Only three significant associations: "toe touch and early hip extension (p = 0.015), bridge on right side with both early hip extension (p = 0.050), and loss of posture (p = 0.028)."
  - Golfers "who could not overhead deep squat or single leg balance on left side… were 2-3 times more likely to exhibit a early hip extension, loss of posture, or slide."

  [Research] — [PubMed 24476744](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24476744/); 2011 poster version: [GVSU ScholarWorks](https://scholarworks.gvsu.edu/ssd/2011/Posters/42)
- **Speariett & Armstrong (2020), J Sport Rehabil:** later critiqued Gulgin et al. for measuring only swing faults, not performance (handicap, clubhead speed). [Research] — [Cumbria repository](https://insight.cumbria.ac.uk/id/eprint/5880)
- **UCF dissertation:** reports good-to-excellent test–retest reliability of the TPI screen, and that screen performance decreased significantly after an 18-hole round. [Research, thesis] — [UCF STARS](https://stars.library.ucf.edu/etd2024/227)
- **Sell, Tsai, Smoliga, Myers & Lephart (2007), J Strength Cond Res 21(4):1166–71.**
  - Sample: 257 healthy male golfers.
  - Finding: the HCP < 0 group had "significantly (p < 0.05) greater hip strength, torso strength, shoulder strength, shoulder flexibility, hip flexibility, torso flexibility, and balance (eyes open) than golfers in the lowest proficiency group (HCP 10–20)."

  [Research] — [Sell 2007 PDF](https://chs.uky.edu/sites/default/files/2023-12/Sell_2007JSCR_Strength%20flexibility%20and%20balance%20characteristics.pdf)
- **2012 study (Int J Sports Phys Ther):** low-handicap golfers "are more likely to have increased pelvis rotation speed as well as increased gluteus maximus and medius strength" than high-handicap golfers. [Research] — [PMC3362989](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3362989)

### Inferences
- **Two characteristics have TPI video recipes complete enough to automate on 2D video**, both DTL:
  - **Over the Top:** address shaft line, plus the "slot" line drawn when the lead forearm is just past parallel in the backswing.
  - **Flat Shoulder Plane:** tailbone→neck line, a parallel line above the trail shoulder, and the lead shoulder position at P4.
- **Flying Elbow** has a qualitative DTL criterion (trail elbow points behind the golfer at P4), but TPI explicitly says it is not necessarily a fault.
- **Only Forward Lunge and Hanging Back** come with TPI "normal" numbers (about 4 in pelvis, 2–3 in upper body). These are usable as rough FO references with a scale (ball diameter 42.67 mm, or a club of known length). TPI cites no measurement study for them.
- **Suggested evidence ranking for the report:**
  1. Gulgin 2014 associations: real, but only three significant.
  2. Sell 2007 group differences: real, but not tied to specific faults.
  3. TPI percentage odds: proprietary; present as "TPI claims."

| Slide | FO | P4 vs P6–P7 |
| Late Buckle | FO and DTL | P7–P8 |
| Reverse Spine Angle | FO (lateral bend toward target), DTL (backward bend) | P4 |
| Forward Lunge | FO | P4 → P6 (head/sternum vs pelvis) |
| Hanging Back | FO | P1 vs P7 (pelvis, weight) |
| Casting | DTL or FO | P5–P6 (wrist angle, lag) |
| Scooping | FO | P7 (clubhead past hands, shaft leaning back) |
| Chicken Winging | FO (and DTL) | P7–P8 |

### Gaps
- TPI's own test videos ("How can I test…") are not transcribed on the pages, so any TPI video criteria inside them could not be read.
- TPI publishes no numeric thresholds for Early Extension, Loss of Posture, Sway or Slide on its public pages. Level 1 course material was not accessible.
- The sample size and full results of Gulgin 2014 could not be read: the PubMed page was blocked by cookies.
- The TPI percentage odds come from a TPI-certified gym, not from a TPI page or study. No peer-reviewed source confirms them.
- The 67% early-extension figure was seen only in a search excerpt of a TPI article; the exact page wording was not re-verified.

## Q2. How do V1, Sportsbox, HackMotion, Skillest and PGA instructors identify Over the Top on video, and how are steep/shallow, across the line/laid off and inside/outside takeaway checked?

### Takeaway
The most precise published OTT criterion is TPI's DTL "slot." The club is over the top if it comes "outside or above" the zone between the address shaft line and the shaft line at lead-forearm-just-past-parallel.
- **V1** only says to check P4 hands and whether the club at P5 is "above or below the backswing plane."
- **HackMotion** gives wrist cues but no video checkpoints.
- **Instructors** use the shaft relative to the trail forearm and the shaft line at the top, but with no validated numbers.
- "Steep" is defined by Top 100 teachers by shaft verticality or attack angle. Being across the line or laid off at the top is **not automatically a fault**.

### Cited Findings

**Over the Top.**
- TPI slot method (DTL, verbatim): see Q1, item 7. [TPI] — [TPI Over the Top](https://www.mytpi.com/improve-my-game/swing-characteristics/over-the-top)
- **V1 Golf:** "The club head appearing outside the hands at the start of the downswing. Best seen in the down-the-line view." Key frames: "P4 (top of backswing) — where are the hands? P5 (early downswing) — is the club above or below the backswing plane? P6 (impact) — steep angle of attack." [Vendor] — [V1: Over-the-Top Analysis](https://swing-analysis.v1sports.com/swing-analysis/over-the-top-analysis). Note that V1 mislabels P6 as impact; see Q5.
- **V1 DTL camera setup:** "Set your phone 8–12 feet behind you, pointing down the target line… Align the camera with your hands at address… Use the line tool to draw the backswing and downswing planes." [Vendor] — [V1: Down-the-Line View](https://swing-analysis.v1sports.com/swing-analysis/down-the-line-view)
- **HackMotion:**
  - OTT "comes steep and across" and "from the outside and across the ball," producing "slices, pulls, or that dreaded pull-slice."
  - Root cause in its view: excess lead-wrist extension at the top, plus an arm-led downswing, poor weight transfer, lack of rotation and early release.
  - No numeric video or wrist thresholds are published in the article.

  [Vendor/Instructor] — [HackMotion: Over the Top](https://hackmotion.com/over-the-top-golf-swing/)
- **Instructor checkpoint:** "If the shaft comes down through the right forearm in the downswing, you are probably going to hit straight shots." [Instructor] — [RotarySwing: shaft/forearm plane](https://rotaryswing.com/golf-instruction/swingfundamentals/golf-shaft-forearm-plane)
- **Trail arm:** when the trail arm is too far in front, it "leads to a more over-the-top swing, resulting in a slice" (Kevin Sprecher). [Instructor] — [GOLF.com](https://golf.com/instruction/common-mistake-trail-arm-contact-kevin-sprecher/)

**Steep vs shallow (Top 100 Teachers roundtable).** [Instructor] — [GOLF.com: "too steep" roundtable](https://golf.com/instruction/too-steep-meaning-top-100-roundtable/)
- **Dom DiJulia:** "In a too-steep swing, the clubhead is traveling more downward than typical (or intended) as it comes into impact." He cites TrackMan: PGA Tour players average about −4.3° attack angle with a 7-iron.
- **Joe Hallett:** "swinging straight down onto the top of the ball rather than swinging into the back of the ball."
- **Brian Mogg:** "the shaft is too vertical on the downswing, and your plane is above a natural path back to impact."
- TPI's Forward Lunge page links an upper-body lunge to "a very steep downward attack angle… very high shots with a lot of spin." [TPI] — [TPI Forward Lunge](https://www.mytpi.com/improve-my-game/swing-characteristics/forward-lunge)
- TPI's Chicken Winging page links steepening and OTT to chicken winging. [TPI] — [TPI Chicken Winging](https://www.mytpi.com/improve-my-game/swing-characteristics/chicken-winging)

**Across the line vs laid off at the top (P4, DTL).**
- Ideally the shaft points "slightly left of your target line at the top" (RH). "Across the line" (pointing right) "frequently requires a difficult compensating move." [Instructor] — [Golf Distillery: shaft position at the top](https://www.golfdistillery.com/swing-tips/top-of-the-swing/shaft-position/)
- A Golf Channel instructor says across the line is the harder position to recover from. [Instructor] — [Golf Channel](https://www.golfchannel.com/news/article-golf-instruction-tip-week-dont-get-across-line-top)
- Laid off "is normally best avoided as it hints to several possible swing flaws." [Instructor] — [Golf Channel](https://www.golfchannel.com/news/article-golf-instruction-tip-week-dont-let-club-get-laid)
- Leslie King's lesson says "many top players don't swing this far back, and are 'laid off' at the top," and calls a laid-off iron position "entirely correct." [Instructor] — [Golf Today: Leslie King lesson 8](https://golftoday.co.uk/leslie-king-tuition-series-an-end-to-trial-error-golf-lesson-8/)
- Mickelson and Daly swing the club across the line at the top. [Instructor/press] — [Australian Golf Digest](https://www.australiangolfdigest.com.au/allow-the-club-to-cross-the-line-to-hit-more-booming-draws/)

**Takeaway (P2, DTL).**
- With the shaft parallel to the ground, "the clubhead should be slightly outside the line of your hands when viewed from down the target line… not behind your body or rolled inside." [Instructor] — [Golf Chicago Magazine instruction (Jul 2025)](https://www.golfchicagomagazine.com/publications/gc_jul_25/GC%202025-4%20instruction.pdf)
- **Rolled inside:** the toe points behind the golfer and the face has rolled open; "the club head will already be way behind your hands, and the shaft will be pointing far to the right of your target." [Instructor/Low quality] — [CaddieHQ: inside takeaway](https://www.caddiehq.com/resources/how-to-fix-inside-takeaway-golf)
- Forum discussion separates "deep hands" from an inside clubhead: deep hands alone don't mean the clubhead is too inside. [Low quality] — [SandTrap: deep hands](https://thesandtrap.com/forums/topic/30325-deep-hands-explained-or-depth/?page=2)

**Quantified shoulder-plane data (useful against Flat Shoulder Plane).** In GOLFTEC's SwingTRU study (30,000 golfers) at the top of the backswing, pros showed 36° of downward shoulder tilt versus 29.6° for high-handicappers (players shooting over 100). [Vendor data] — [Golf Digest ME: SwingTRU 6 secrets](https://golfdigestme.com/swing-numbers-new-study-unlocks-6-swing-secrets/)

### Inferences
- **A checkable 2D OTT protocol (DTL, camera at hand height on the hands–target line), from TPI:**
  1. Line A = shaft line at P1.
  2. Line B = shaft line at the backswing frame where the lead forearm is just past parallel (about P3).
  3. In the downswing (P5–P6), if the clubhead or shaft rises **above/outside Line A** (outside the A–B slot) → Over the Top.

  Secondary instructor cues:
  - at P6 the shaft is visibly above or outside the trail forearm, or points well outside the ball/target line;
  - the clubhead appears outside the hands early in the downswing (V1).

  The secondary cues are not validated.
- **Steep vs shallow from DTL:** compare the shaft angle in early downswing with the P1 shaft line. Above it means steepening, under the hands-plane means shallow or "stuck." There is no validated degree threshold. One unattributed blog's "8–10°" figure is not usable.
- **Across the line and laid off** should be reported as descriptive P4 shaft directions, not faults. Flag them only if the downswing then fails, for example by coming over the top or getting stuck.
- **2D caveat:** camera height and alignment change apparent plane angles. Lines are only comparable within one camera setup, so the report should require the V1 setup (camera aligned with the hands at address).

### Gaps
- The Sportsbox AI official knowledge base (its definitions of club plane and hand path) was not reachable. No official Sportsbox OTT criterion was found.
- No Skillest-specific OTT checkpoint was found.
- No peer-reviewed study gives a validated 2D threshold (in degrees or cm) for OTT, steepness, or "shaft above the trail forearm at P6."
- HackMotion's numeric wrist ranges for OTT are not in the article (they may be in its app or data reports).

## Q3. How are Early Extension, Sway and Slide (and Hanging Back, Forward Lunge, Reverse Pivot) measured, and what thresholds exist?

### Takeaway
- **Early Extension:** there is no published, validated cm threshold. TPI defines it qualitatively (any pelvis movement toward the ball), and V1 checks hip position at P4 and P6 versus setup. Sportsbox exposes a "thrust" (depth) metric, but its tour norms weren't found.
- **Sway and slide:** instructors use vertical lines on FO video (outside of the trail foot or trail hip for sway, the lead hip for slide). The only quantitative anchors are vendor data (GOLFTEC) and TPI's rule-of-thumb inches, and these sources disagree on backswing sway.

### Cited Findings

**Early Extension.**
- **V1:** "The hips moving toward the ball (losing the tilt) in the downswing. Best seen from face-on view. Key frames: P4 — hip position relative to setup; P6 — has the hip line changed? Are they closer to the ball?" [Vendor] — [V1: Early Extension](https://swing-analysis.v1sports.com/swing-analysis/early-extension-analysis). V1 says face-on here, while pelvis-to-ball depth is usually judged DTL (see Inferences).
- **Sportsbox via GOLF.com:** the "Chest Bend" number can help monitor early extension: bending at the hips keeps the pelvis back and reduces early extension. The reviewer's chest bend at the top was about −6°, "right at the edge of the range." [Vendor/press] — [GOLF.com: Sportsbox exposed flaws](https://golf.com/instruction/sportsbox-ai-exposed-flaws-golf-swing-play-smart/)
- **"Thrust"** (third-party guide using Sportsbox 3D captures): "how far each segment (pelvis, chest, hands, club, head) has moved toward (+) or away (−) from the ball." It is reported at the top, at mid-downswing (club horizontal) and at impact, and used to diagnose "early extension, posture loss, and depth-driven path issues." [Low quality: not affiliated with Sportsbox] — [vectorgolf-ai metrics](https://vectorgolf-ai.pages.dev/metrics/)
- **Pelvic lift** is a separate Sportsbox metric for vertical force. "you need to first squat into the ground (lowering the pelvis) and then push up off the ground (raising the pelvis)." The best players "drop their pelvis during the downswing and then lift it back up before impact." [Vendor/press] — [GOLF.com: pelvic lift (Sportsbox)](https://golf.com/instruction/driving/pelvic-lift-generate-power-sportsbox-ai/)
- The posterior pelvic tilt reference in the Pelvic Tilt row of the Q1 table applies here: "Top level players can tilt their pelvis backwards by up to 20 degrees during the downswing." [TPI, second-hand] — [Pinnacle: Pelvic Tilt](https://pinnaclefitness.squarespace.com/blog/the-power-of-golf-tpi-screen-pelvic-tilt)
- **Peer-reviewed link:** toe-touch failure was associated with early hip extension (p = 0.015). Failing the right-side bridge was associated with both early hip extension (p = 0.050) and loss of posture (p = 0.028). [Research] — [Gulgin 2014, PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24476744/)

**Sway (backswing).**
- **TPI:** "forces your weight to the outside of your trail side foot." [TPI] — [TPI Sway](https://www.mytpi.com/improve-my-game/swing-characteristics/sway)
- **V1:** "The right hip moving laterally past the right foot in the backswing. Best seen from face-on." Key frames P2–P4. [Vendor] — [V1: Hip Sway](https://swing-analysis.v1sports.com/swing-analysis/hip-sway-analysis)
- **Line method:** "Draw an imaginary vertical line up from the outside of your trail foot… Does [the trail hip] move significantly outside of that line? If so, you've got a sway." [Instructor/Low quality] — [CaddieHQ: what is a sway](https://www.caddiehq.com/resources/what-is-a-sway-in-golf)
- **FO camera setup:** "Set your phone parallel to the ball-target line, 6–10 feet in front of you… Record at hip height." The FO view "reveals hip sway, early extension, reverse pivot, and weight transfer." [Vendor] — [V1: Face-On View](https://swing-analysis.v1sports.com/swing-analysis/face-on-view)
- **Tour backswing data (Nick Clearwater, GOLFTEC):** "Tour players do very little of it in the backswing."
  - At lead-arm parallel, the hips are 0.3 in away from the target and the shoulders 0.1 in away.
  - From there to the top, the hips move about 0.5 in toward the target, so elite players end the backswing "slightly closer to the target than they were at address."

  [Vendor data] — [GOLF.com: Clearwater on hip sway](https://golf.com/instruction/nick-clearwater-calrifies-hip-sway-misunderstanding/)
- **Conflicting GOLFTEC SwingTRU data:** "The tour pro… has an average hip movement of 3.9 inches toward the target at the top of the backswing" versus 2.3 in for a 30-handicapper. [Vendor data] — [GOLFTEC blog: hip movement](https://scramble.golftec.com/blog/2016/09/hip-movement-good-golf-swing/). Golf Digest ME reports 3.9 in (pros) versus 2.55 in (high-handicappers). [Vendor data] — [Golf Digest ME](https://golfdigestme.com/swing-numbers-new-study-unlocks-6-swing-secrets/). This contradicts Clearwater's figure of about 0.2 in. The likely difference is sensor or reference definitions (unconfirmed).

**Slide and weight shift (downswing).**
- **TPI Slide:** "excessive lower body lateral movement towards the target during your downswing." [TPI] — [TPI Slide](https://www.mytpi.com/improve-my-game/swing-characteristics/slide)
- **Line method:** "a little bit of lateral motion is okay and expected - this is the weight shift. But if you see your lead hip crash well past that line… you're sliding." [Instructor/Low quality] — [CaddieHQ: hip slide](https://www.caddiehq.com/resources/how-to-stop-hip-slide-in-golf-swing)
- **Clearwater:** from the top to impact, tour players shift the hip center about 3 in toward the target, "considerably more than what average golfers do." [Vendor data] — [GOLF.com: Clearwater](https://golf.com/instruction/nick-clearwater-calrifies-hip-sway-misunderstanding/)
- **SwingTRU at impact:** hips 1.6 in toward the target versus address for pros, but 0.4 in *away* from the target for high-handicappers. Hips open 36° at impact for pros versus 19.5°. [Vendor data] — [Golf Digest ME](https://golfdigestme.com/swing-numbers-new-study-unlocks-6-swing-secrets/)
- **TPI Hanging Back:** "In a normal weight shift, the player is about four inches closer to the target at impact compared to address." [TPI] — [TPI Hanging Back](https://www.mytpi.com/improve-my-game/swing-characteristics/hanging-back)
- **TPI Forward Lunge:** normal is about 4 in of lower-body shift and 2–3 in of upper body. A lunge is when the upper body moves more than the lower body. [TPI] — [TPI Forward Lunge](https://www.mytpi.com/improve-my-game/swing-characteristics/forward-lunge)
- **Reverse pivot:** "Weight moves to the front foot on the backswing and back foot on the downswing." Key frames: "P4 — which way is the spine tilting? P6 — is weight on the front foot at impact?" [Vendor] — [V1: Reverse Pivot](https://swing-analysis.v1sports.com/swing-analysis/reverse-pivot-analysis). TPI gives the same definition inside Hanging Back. [TPI] — [TPI Hanging Back](https://www.mytpi.com/improve-my-game/swing-characteristics/hanging-back)
- **Shoulder tilt at impact (SwingTRU):** 39° upward for pros versus 27.5° for high-handicappers. Shoulder "bend" (torso leaning away from the target) in the follow-through: 32° for pros versus 3.2°. [Vendor data] — [Golf Digest ME](https://golfdigestme.com/swing-numbers-new-study-unlocks-6-swing-secrets/)

### Inferences
- **Early extension on 2D DTL video:** the practical, checkable criterion follows TPI's definition. Draw a vertical reference line touching the trail buttock at P1 (the "tush line"). Early extension means the pelvis, especially the trail buttock, comes off that line toward the ball between P4/P5 and P7.
  - Two refinements separate it from tour-like extension: (a) whether the pelvis moves toward the ball versus upward and back (pelvic lift with posterior tilt), and (b) whether the hip-to-ball distance shrinks at P6–P7 compared with P1.
  - Converting pixels to cm requires a scale, such as the ball (4.267 cm) or the known club length. Since no validated threshold exists, the report should express it as a relative change (for example, "buttock X cm in front of the P1 line at P7") and grade it, not pass/fail it.
  - The "tush line" name is common usage. No authoritative primary source was found in this search.
- **Sway (FO, camera on the ball line at hip height):** the vertical line at the outside of the trail foot, or the trail hip at P1, is a checkable instructor criterion. Tour data suggest the pelvis center should not end up away from the target at P4 (Clearwater), so a pelvis center clearly away from the target at P4, beyond the trail-foot line, is a strong sway signal. Use GOLFTEC/TPI inch numbers only as context because of the conflicts above.
- **Slide (FO):** some lateral pelvis movement toward the target is expected: about 1.6 in vs address at impact (SwingTRU), about 3 in from top to impact (Clearwater), about 4 in per TPI. Flag a slide when the lead hip moves clearly past a vertical line at the lead hip or outside of the lead foot, **combined with** little hip rotation (pros: about 36° open at impact). Lateral movement alone isn't enough.
- **Forward lunge (FO):** compare the head or sternum lateral shift with the pelvis shift from P4 to P6. Upper body > pelvis indicates a lunge (TPI's rule).
- **Hanging back (FO):** pelvis at P7 not ahead of its P1 position, especially behind it (high-handicap average −0.4 in), plus trail-heavy weight or a trail knee not driving.

### Gaps
- Official Sportsbox definitions and tour norms for pelvis thrust, sway and lift (in cm or inches at P4, P6 and P7) were not found. The Sportsbox knowledge base was not reachable.
- No peer-reviewed numeric threshold (in cm) exists for early extension, sway or slide on 2D video.
- The conflict between the SwingTRU hip sway at the top (3.9 in toward the target) and Clearwater's roughly 0.2 in is unresolved. The measurement definitions are not published.
- A reported Sportsbox-based figure ("most golfers, including Rory McIlroy, sway their hips toward the target up to six inches by impact") could not be traced to a confirmed URL. Do not use it.

## Q4. What distinguishes legitimate tour patterns from faults, so the analysis doesn't call normal things faults?

### Takeaway
Several "faults" are explicitly not faults per se:
- TPI says this of Flying Elbow, and calls all 16 "characteristics."
- Some top players are across the line (Mickelson, Daly) or laid off.
- Tour players move laterally toward the target in the downswing, lift the pelvis before impact, tilt the pelvis posteriorly, and keep much more shoulder tilt than amateurs.

The fault judgment should hinge on the **outcome**: path, face and contact, plus whether a compensation is needed. A deviation from a model by itself is not a fault.

### Cited Findings
- **TPI:** characteristics, not faults, because they don't "preclude players from playing great golf"; some golfers early extend because "it's the most effective way for them to swing based on their physical tools." [TPI] — [TPI article](https://www.mytpi.com/articles/swing/early-extension-swing-characteristic)
- **Flying Elbow:** "not necessarily a fault and many great players play golf with this motion… many great players have a flying elbow and still manage to get the arm in the correct position on the downswing." [TPI] — [TPI Flying Elbow](https://www.mytpi.com/improve-my-game/swing-characteristics/flying-elbow)
- **The early-extension debate:**
  - Some teachers say extension "may be a player's own way to generate power," citing Jack Nicklaus.
  - Peter Kostis tweeted: "So much for early extension being terrible for your swing!"
  - The article itself defines early extension as "when your pelvis moves towards the ball during your downswing."

  [Press/Instructor] — [GOLF.com: Is early extension good or bad?](https://golf.com/instruction/is-early-extension-good-or-bad-heres-what-you-need-to-know/)
- **Ground force, not a fault:** the best players "drop their pelvis during the downswing and then lift it back up before impact" (pelvic lift). [Vendor/press] — [GOLF.com: pelvic lift (Sportsbox)](https://golf.com/instruction/driving/pelvic-lift-generate-power-sportsbox-ai/)
- **Normal lateral shift:** tour players shift the hips about 3 in toward the target from the top to impact. [Vendor data] — [GOLF.com: Clearwater](https://golf.com/instruction/nick-clearwater-calrifies-hip-sway-misunderstanding/). TPI says about 4 in. [TPI] — [TPI Hanging Back](https://www.mytpi.com/improve-my-game/swing-characteristics/hanging-back)
- **Normal "standing up" after impact:** "Normally, after a player makes contact with the ball and the club releases around their body, there is a corresponding lift or standing tall into the finish position." Only a dip or knee buckle after impact is the characteristic. [TPI] — [TPI Late Buckle](https://www.mytpi.com/improve-my-game/swing-characteristics/late-buckle)
- **Shoulder tilt and side bend are larger in pros:**
  - 36° downward at the top;
  - 39° upward at impact;
  - 32° of torso bend away from the target in the follow-through, versus 3.2° in high-handicappers.

  The high-handicap posture is *more* "level." [Vendor data] — [Golf Digest ME: SwingTRU](https://golfdigestme.com/swing-numbers-new-study-unlocks-6-swing-secrets/)
- **Across the line and laid off:**
  - Some instructors say it's "completely fine to have that club a little bit across the line at the top as long as the player can shallow it on the way down."
  - Many top players are laid off, and laid off can be "entirely correct" with irons.

  [Instructor] — [Golf Today: Leslie King](https://golftoday.co.uk/leslie-king-tuition-series-an-end-to-trial-error-golf-lesson-8/); [Australian Golf Digest](https://www.australiangolfdigest.com.au/allow-the-club-to-cross-the-line-to-hit-more-booming-draws/)
- **Average models aren't ideals:** a commenter on the SwingTRU data notes that these are averages across all body types and swing styles. [Low quality] — [SandTrap: GolfTEC numbers thread](https://thesandtrap.com/forums/topic/94631-golftec-numbers-golf-digest-aug-17/)
- **Steepness has a normal range:** tour players hit down about 4.3° with a 7-iron (TrackMan), so "steep" means more downward than intended, not any downward strike. [Instructor] — [GOLF.com: Top 100 roundtable](https://golf.com/instruction/too-steep-meaning-top-100-roundtable/)

### Inferences
- **Rules to avoid false positives:**
  1. Report "loss of posture" only for changes before impact (P4–P7). Rising after impact is normal (TPI Late Buckle text).
  2. Separate pelvis **upward and back** (lift, posterior tilt: tour-like) from pelvis **toward the ball** (early extension). On DTL, judge the horizontal distance of the trail buttock from the P1 line, not the head or pelvis height.
  3. Lateral pelvis movement toward the target in the downswing is normal. Flag a slide only if it is excessive *and* rotation is poor.
  4. Flying elbow, across the line and laid off are descriptive. Flag them only when linked to a downstream problem (OTT, stuck, face control).
  5. Tilted shoulders at the top (lead shoulder well below the trail shoulder from FO) is tour-like. "Level" shoulders are the amateur pattern, consistent with the TPI Flat Shoulder Plane definition.
- **Tie every fault claim to a consequence visible in the ball flight or contact the follower reports**, or in the video (divot position, strike). Otherwise label it a "characteristic" rather than a "fault."

### Gaps
- No peer-reviewed source quantifies how much tour players' pelvis-to-ball distance changes from P1 to P7, the "acceptable" early-extension range.
- Heel lift in the backswing (a tour pattern in some players) was not covered by the sources found.

## Q5. What are the P1–P10 definitions in the most common system, and which alternative numberings exist?

### Takeaway
The common "P classification" defines ten checkpoints by shaft or lead-arm parallel positions. Sources agree on P1, P3, P4, P5, P7, P9 and P10, and vary on P2, P6 and P8. V1's web guides use a **non-standard** labelling (P6 = impact). One Keiser University author places P6 at lead-arm-parallel. The report should state its definitions explicitly.

### Cited Findings
- **HackMotion definitions.** [Vendor] — [HackMotion: Golf swing positions](https://hackmotion.com/golf-swing-positions/)

| Position | Definition |
|---|---|
| P1 | Address |
| P2 | Takeaway, "ends when the club is parallel to the ground" |
| P3 | Halfway back, "lead arm parallel to the ground" |
| P4 | Top of the backswing |
| P5 | Early downswing, "from the top… to the club parallel" (other sources: lead arm parallel in the downswing) |
| P6 | Pre-impact, "shaft parallel to the ground before impact" |
| P7 | Impact |
| P8 | Release, after impact: arms fully extended, trail arm rotating over |
