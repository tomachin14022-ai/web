## Q1. Which drills are most cited for each fault, with exact steps, feedback and progression?

### Takeaway
For the classic faults, credible sources agree on a small set of drills. The best documented are:
- the wall or chair-behind-the-glutes drill for early extension;
- the alignment stick outside the trail foot for sway;
- a towel under the armpits for chicken wing;
- "hit hard, stop quick" and lower-body-first drills for casting and flipping;
- shallowing and trail-pocket drills for over the top.

Step-by-step instructions are well published for over the top, early extension and casting/flip (HackMotion, GOLF.com/Chris Como). TPI names drills for each characteristic (e.g. "Step Change", "Picket Fence", "Get Closer"), but its public pages give no written steps, only videos. Progressions follow the same pattern everywhere: slow rehearsal with no ball → half swing → full swing → ball → speed. HackMotion and GOLF.com state this explicitly.

### Cited Findings

**General progression principles**
- **[Vendor] HackMotion app levels for over the top / release:** "Level 1: Release drill → Level 2: Dynamic transition drill → Level 3: Impact full swing challenge", and "Train your release before working on other aspects of your swing". — [HackMotion, 7 Drills to Stop Coming Over the Top](https://hackmotion.com/drills-to-stop-coming-over-the-top/)
- **[Vendor] Recurring progression language in HackMotion drills:**
  - "Start slow with this, and then eventually add a little speed."
  - "Gradually increase speed as you get comfortable with the motion."
  - Water bottle drill: "Repeat 10–15 times before hitting shots with the same feel."
  - Source: [HackMotion OTT](https://hackmotion.com/drills-to-stop-coming-over-the-top/)
- **[Vendor] Casting drills progression:** small swings → longer swings, slow → moderate speed, "add speed only when alignment holds". Rehearsal without a ball (club upside down), then "repeat with a ball, removing or imagining the object". — [HackMotion, 6 Drills to Stop Casting](https://hackmotion.com/drills-to-stop-casting/)
- **[TPI] Body before drills:** TPI's Over the Top page recommends starting with hip and spine mobility work, then progressing to weight-shift sequencing drills. — [TPI Over the Top](https://cdn.site.mytpi.com/improve-my-game/swing-characteristics/over-the-top)
- **GOLF.com on the towel drill:** the chicken-wing towel drill works best on half swings, since swinging too big makes solid contact harder (search summary, page not fetched). — [GOLF.com, drill golf chicken wing fix](https://golf.com/instruction/drill-golf-chicken-wing-fix/?amp=1)

**1. Over the top (OTT / "por afuera", path outside-in)**
- **[TPI] Definition:** a downswing fault in which "the upper body takes over the downswing, throwing the club outside the intended swing plane". Result: a pull with a square face, or a slice with an open face.
  - **Diagnosis (down the line):** compare the club against a "slot" drawn from the shaft plane and the lead forearm. If the club passes above/outside the slot in the downswing, the fault is present.
  - **TPI drills (named only, no written steps):** Step Change (lower body leads the downswing); Side Arm Throw (drop the trail elbow to the side in transition); Barriers (balls/tees as obstacles).
  - Source: [TPI Over the Top](https://cdn.site.mytpi.com/improve-my-game/swing-characteristics/over-the-top)
- **[Vendor] HackMotion's 7 drills**, with steps quoted from the source (no individual coach credited). Source: [HackMotion OTT](https://hackmotion.com/drills-to-stop-coming-over-the-top/)
  1. **Trail Foot Flare.**
     - Steps: flare the trail foot "15–25° open" → more hip rotation and depth in the backswing → "easier to bring the club down on an inside path"; practice swings focusing on a deeper turn.
  2. **Wrist Position Fix.**
     - Steps: at the top, check the lead wrist. If it is "overly extended (cupped), you will likely come over the top"; aim for "a flat lead wrist or slight flexion"; slow swings.
     - No-sensor version: hold a book between the palms, point it to the sky at the top (lead wrist flexed), then turn it down toward the ground in the downswing.
  3. **Deep Backswing.**
     - Steps: at the top, the lead arm is in line with the shoulder plane and "the butt of the club should be over your ankle line, not your toe line"; if the hands are too high, turn more; a slight trail-leg setback helps you feel it.
  4. **Hands to the Pocket Transition.**
     - Steps: in transition, the hands move "towards your trail pocket rather than out in front of you", with the shoulders kept closed; add speed gradually.
  5. **Shoulder Blade Control.**
     - Steps: in posture, pull the trail shoulder back and reach the lead shoulder forward; hold; in transition, "your back stays facing the target longer while your hips begin to open".
  6. **Water Bottle.**
     - Steps: a quarter-filled bottle in the trail hand; at transition, "pour the water out behind you (instead of toward the ground)" → a shallower arm drop; 10–15 reps, then hit shots with the same feel.
  7. **Motorcycle.**
     - Steps: at the top, "twist your lead wrist downward as if you're slowing down a motorcycle throttle" → lead wrist flexion, trail wrist extension; slow motion, stopping at the top and at P6 (shaft parallel in the downswing).
  - **Caveat:** the same article contradicts itself. It lists "flat or slightly flexed" lead wrist at the top as a problem and also as the goal. Cite it carefully. — [HackMotion OTT](https://hackmotion.com/drills-to-stop-coming-over-the-top/)
- **[Opinion, GOLF Top 100 teacher] Load the trail hip first:** load the trail hip in the backswing before shifting left. This makes room for the arms to travel more from the inside (search summary, page not fetched). — [GOLF.com, load hip get rid of slice](https://golf.com/instruction/load-hip-get-rid-of-slice/?amp=1)

**2. Early extension ("extensión temprana", pelvis/hips move toward the ball)**
- **[TPI] Definition:** "any forward movement (thrust) of the lower body towards the golf ball during the downswing".
  - **TPI swing drills (named, no written steps):** "Get Closer" (forces you to start moving away from the ball); "Picket Fence" (rotate and shift toward the target without moving closer to the ball).
  - Source: [TPI Early Extension](https://cdn.site.mytpi.com/improve-my-game/swing-characteristics/early-extension)
- **[Consensus] Wall/chair behind the glutes:** set up with the glutes lightly touching a wall, chair or bag, and swing slowly while keeping contact.
  - **Feedback:** if your butt leaves the line before impact (a gap opens), that is early extension.
  - Cited by GOLF.com as "a common drill used by our GOLF Top 100 Teachers".
  - Sources: [GOLF.com, easy drill prevent early extension](https://golf.com/instruction/use-easy-drill-prevent-early-extension-in-your-swing/); [GolfLink](https://golflink.com/instruction/stop-early-extension); [Bryn Parry Golf](https://newsroom.prkarma.com/brynparrygolf/news_release/1551)
  - **Caveat (forum):** keeping the chair there does not by itself guarantee correct forward hip motion. — [TheSandTrap forum](https://thesandtrap.com/forums/topic/41526-maintaining-butts-position-throughout-downswing)
- **[Opinion, Chris Como via GOLF.com] Chair-in-front knee drill** (targets too much knee flex as a cause of early extension):
  - **Basic version:** chair seat facing you, knees "no more than an inch away" from the seat edge, arms crossed on the chest; turn back and through without hitting the chair.
  - **Feedback:** too much flex → the knees collapse toward the ball and "clip the chair on the way through".
  - **Progression:** start with the knees 4–5 in from the seat; as you start down, feel the knees move closer to the chair; through impact, stand up so the knees move away while the chest stays over the ball.
  - **Success check:** the legs finish "close together, almost touching".
  - Source: [GOLF.com, Chris Como chair drill](https://golf.com/instruction/chair-drill-cure-early-extension-chris-como)
- **Push-cart variation:** in transition, push a cart back with your butt (search summary, page not fetched). — [GOLF.com, early extension how to fix](https://golf.com/instruction/early-extension-golf-swing-how-to-fix)
- **[Vendor] Other HackMotion fixes for early extension:** "hit hard, stop quick" with a short/mid iron and a short-to-medium backswing; an alignment-stick version of the glute-contact drill (search summary). — [HackMotion, 7 fixes early extension](https://hackmotion.com/how-to-stop-early-extension-in-golf)

**3. Loss of posture / standing up ("pérdida de postura")**
- **[TPI] Definition:** "any significant alteration from your body's original set up angles during your golf swing".
  - **Drills (named, no steps):** "Sweep the Dust" (keeps a good shoulder plane in the backswing); "Loss of Posture Drill" (keeps trail-leg flex).
  - Source: [TPI Loss of Posture](https://cdn.site.mytpi.com/improve-my-game/swing-characteristics/loss-of-posture)
- **[TPI] Related characteristic, Flat Shoulder Plane:** standing up in the backswing, which flattens the shoulder plane. — [TPI swing characteristics index](https://cdn.site.mytpi.com/improve-my-game/swing-characteristics)

**4. Sway (lateral move away from the target in the backswing)**
- **[TPI] Definition:** "any excessive lower body lateral movement away from the target during your backswing". TPI's sway page lists only exercises (Q2), not technical drills. — [TPI Sway](https://cdn.site.mytpi.com/improve-my-game/swing-characteristics/sway)
- **[Consensus] Alignment stick outside the trail foot:**
  - **Steps:** a stick in the ground just outside the trail foot, angled slightly; feel the hip turn away from the stick, back and deeper.
  - **Feedback:** if you sway, the hip bumps the stick.
  - **Variant:** a stick from the outside of the shoe up to the outside of the hip.
  - Sources (search summary): [GolfMagic, how to fix a sway](https://www.golfmagic.com/best-tips-and-instruction/step-2-how-fix-sway); [CaddieHQ](https://www.caddiehq.com/resources/how-to-stop-swaying-in-golf-swing)
- **Split-stance drill:**
  - **Steps:** step the trail foot straight back 12–18 in, toe on the ground for balance; hit easy half shots rotating around the front leg.
  - **Feedback:** it is "almost impossible to sway onto your back foot" in this position.
  - Source: search summary of the same result set (GolfMagic/CaddieHQ); exact page not verified.

**5. Slide (lateral move toward the target in the downswing) and Late Buckle**
- **[TPI] Definitions:** Slide is "excessive lower-body lateral movement toward the target in the downswing". Late Buckle is "similar to a slide, but it happens after impact". — [TPI index](https://cdn.site.mytpi.com/improve-my-game/swing-characteristics)
- **Note:** one search summary said TPI pages "don't clearly separate sway from slide". The fetched TPI index and Sway page do separate them: sway = backswing, away from target; slide = downswing, toward target. Trust the fetched pages.

**6. Reverse spine angle ("ángulo de columna invertido")**
- **[TPI] Description:** "the number one cause of lower back pain in golf". — [TPI index](https://cdn.site.mytpi.com/improve-my-game/swing-characteristics)
- **[TPI-style, secondary] Definition:** "excessive upper body backward bend or excessive left lateral (for right handed golfers) upper body bend during the backswing". — [Miami Valley Golf Assn. reproducing TPI content](https://www.miamivalleygolf.org/about/partners/visit-our-partners-web-sites/reverse-spine-angle-3474c7928.html)
- **[Opinion] Links to other faults:** if the body can't rotate around the hip, a lateral sway may occur, and a sway "will force the spine to tilt into backward bend". A reverse spine angle causing early extension in the downswing is common. — [Central Links Golf](https://centrallinksgolf.org/what-are-the-top-2-causes-of-low-back-pain-in-golf); [GolfWRX, reverse spine angle](https://www.golfwrx.com/199983/three-common-swing-faults-reverse-spine-angle/)

**7. Flying elbow / across the line ("palo cruzado")**
- **[TPI] Flying Elbow definition:** "an extremely raised trail elbow during the backswing". — [TPI index](https://cdn.site.mytpi.com/improve-my-game/swing-characteristics)
- **Across-the-line drills** (search summary, pages not fetched):
  - **Club across the shoulders:** keep the clubhead in line with the hands at the top.
  - **Headcover Tuck:** a headcover under the trail armpit. "If you just lift your arms, the headcover will drop immediately."
  - **Waiter's Tray:** trail palm under the club like carrying a tray.
  - Sources: [Rotary Swing glossary, Across the Line](https://rotaryswing.com/glossary/across-the-line); [CaddieHQ, fix across the line](https://www.caddiehq.com/resources/how-to-fix-across-the-line-golf-swing)
- Sources link flying elbow + overswing + across the line together (title only). — [RotarySwing, Fix Overswing/Across the Line – Flying Right Elbow](https://rotaryswing.com/c4/379-fix-overswing-across-the-line-flying-right-elbow)

**8. Inside takeaway / laid off**
- **Cause:** "A laid-off swing primarily starts with a takeaway that is too inside" (search summary). — [HackMotion, Laid-Off Golf Swing](https://hackmotion.com/laid-off-golf-swing/)
- **Drill:** normal backswing, keeping the shaft more vertical at the top, with alignment sticks as a visual reference (search summary). — [Rotary Swing glossary, laid off](https://rotaryswing.com/glossary/laid-off)
- **[Opinion, tour player] Scottie Scheffler's "pencil drill":** aimed at amateurs who get laid off at the top (search summary; steps not retrieved). — [Golf365](https://www.golf365.com/golf-instruction/scottie-scheffler-pencil-drill-driving-tips-golf-driving)
- **[Vendor] Deep Backswing drill** (see OTT #3): checks depth without getting the hands high or outside. — [HackMotion OTT](https://hackmotion.com/drills-to-stop-coming-over-the-top/)

**9. Casting / early release**
- **[TPI] Definition:** "an early release of the club, named after a fishing cast". — [TPI index](https://cdn.site.mytpi.com/improve-my-game/swing-characteristics)
- **[Vendor] HackMotion's 6 drills** (no individual coach credited). Source: [HackMotion Casting](https://hackmotion.com/drills-to-stop-casting/)
  1. **Hit Hard, Stop Quick.**
     - Steps: half or three-quarter backswing, firm downswing, stop the club right after contact with the hands ahead of the clubhead.
     - Feedback: if the shaft has flipped past the hands, reset.
  2. **Motorcycle.**
     - Steps: add lead-wrist flexion in the downswing.
     - Feedback: hooks mean too much bowing; persistent slices mean more flexion is needed.
  3. **HackMotion Casting Drill.**
     - Steps: slow motion from the top; shift weight and rotate the hips without pulling down with the arms; keep the trail wrist bent back; the trail arm straightens only after the hips unwind.
  4. **Body-First Anti-Cast.**
     - Steps: a chair or box waist-high, just in front of and slightly outside the hands; club upside down; pause at the top in an L shape; bump the hips first; clear the object with the body; release after. Then repeat with a ball, removing or imagining the object.
     - Feedback: casting hits the object.
  5. **Stronger Clubface.**
     - Steps: stronger grip; flat lead wrist to the top; the face "looks" toward the ground in the takeaway.
     - Checkpoint: the shaft is still parallel to the ground when the hands reach the trail thigh.
  6. **Lower Body Priority.**
     - Steps: waist-high to waist-high, "body first, arms second"; progress to full swings checking forward shaft lean.
  - **Stated causes:** an overly open face at the top; the upper body without lower-body lead; hands and arms taking over. — [HackMotion Casting](https://hackmotion.com/drills-to-stop-casting/)
- **[Vendor] App tip:** a trail-wrist extension that straightens too soon in the downswing graph signals casting (search summary). — [HackMotion](https://hackmotion.com/drills-to-stop-casting/)

**10. Scooping / flip**
- **[TPI] Definition:** scooping "often occurs with casting, possibly from trying to lift the ball or add loft". — [TPI index](https://cdn.site.mytpi.com/improve-my-game/swing-characteristics)
- **[Vendor] HackMotion's view:** excessive lead-wrist extension is the root cause of a flip. The fix is a flatter lead wrist at the top so the face squares sooner ("waiting too long leaves a flip as the only option"). Recommended drill: Motorcycle (search summary). — [HackMotion, Stop Flipping Wrists](https://hackmotion.com/stop-flipping-wrists-with-hackmotion/)
- **Independent check of the sensor:** a GolfLink reviewer cut their over-the-top path from 5°+ to ≤2°, but the sensor "still flagged them for casting on nearly every swing" (search summary). — [GolfLink HackMotion review](https://www.golflink.com/equipment/hackmotion-review-3432639)

**11. Chicken wing (lead arm breaks down through impact)**
- **[TPI] Definition:** "a breakdown of the lead arm through impact that can limit speed or power". — [TPI index](https://cdn.site.mytpi.com/improve-my-game/swing-characteristics)
- **[Consensus] Towel under both armpits:**
  - **Steps:** slow half swings, keeping the towel in place from takeaway to finish.
  - **Feedback:** if it drops early, the arms have disconnected from the torso.
  - Sources (search summary): [GOLF.com, drill chicken wing fix](https://golf.com/instruction/drill-golf-chicken-wing-fix/?amp=1); [Yahoo/Golf Digest AU, stop chicken winging or flipping](https://au.sports.yahoo.com/golf-instruction-try-stop-chicken-140035318.html)

**12. Hanging back / poor weight shift**
- **[TPI] Hanging Back definition:** "a complete lack of forward weight shift in the downswing". **Forward Lunge:** "an aggressive upper-body lunge toward the target during transition". — [TPI index](https://cdn.site.mytpi.com/improve-my-game/swing-characteristics)
- **[TPI] Drills and exercises:** Step Change (lower body leads the downswing); "Change of Direction" exercise (promotes forward weight shift and dynamic upper–lower separation). — [TPI Over the Top](https://cdn.site.mytpi.com/improve-my-game/swing-characteristics/over-the-top); [TPI Sway](https://cdn.site.mytpi.com/improve-my-game/swing-characteristics/sway)
- **[Vendor] Shift first:** the HackMotion Casting Drill and Body-First Anti-Cast both start the downswing by shifting weight and bumping the hips toward the target. — [HackMotion Casting](https://hackmotion.com/drills-to-stop-casting/)
- **Split-stance drill** (see Sway) is also described for rotating around the front leg (search summary; page not verified).

**13. Steep vs. shallow downswing**
- **Steep / OTT arm path:** Water Bottle, Hands-to-Pocket, Shoulder Blade Control (HackMotion); Side Arm Throw (TPI, named only). — [HackMotion OTT](https://hackmotion.com/drills-to-stop-coming-over-the-top/); [TPI OTT](https://cdn.site.mytpi.com/improve-my-game/swing-characteristics/over-the-top)
- **[TPI] Early extension steepens the downswing plane:** TPI calls it "one of the leading causes of slicing" (search summary of the TPI page). — [TPI swing characteristics](https://cdn.site.mytpi.com/improve-my-game/swing-characteristics)
- **Too shallow / stuck:** no credible drill retrieved (see Gaps).

### Inferences
- The most defensible drill per fault for a short reel ending with "one drill":
  - early extension → glutes-to-wall/chair;
  - sway → stick outside the trail foot;
  - chicken wing → towel under armpits on half swings;
  - casting/flip → "hit hard, stop quick" or the lower-body-first L-to-L rehearsal;
  - OTT → hands-to-trail-pocket or water bottle;
  - across the line → headcover under the trail arm.
  These have clear steps and built-in feedback (a contact or a falling object), and several sources describe them.
- TPI's named drills (Step Change, Picket Fence, Get Closer, Sweep the Dust, Side Arm Throw) carry TPI's brand authority, but their exact steps are only in TPI videos. Don't describe their steps from memory; cite only the name and purpose.
- A sound generic progression (supported by HackMotion + TPI + GOLF.com): mobility/rehearsal without a club or ball → slow motion with pauses at checkpoints (top, P6) → half swing (waist to waist) → full swing → ball → speed. Move up only when the feedback (contact, sensor, ball flight) stays correct.

### Gaps
- No primary written steps found for these TPI drills: Step Change, Side Arm Throw, Barriers, Get Closer, Picket Fence, Sweep the Dust, Loss of Posture Drill. TPI shows them only in video.
- Not retrieved or verified with full steps:
  - drills for slide (e.g. a stick outside the lead hip);
  - drills for reverse spine angle;
  - a too-shallow / "stuck" downswing;
  - the inside takeaway specifically (e.g. a stick or headcover outside the ball in the takeaway);
  - the Scheffler pencil drill steps.
  Commonly known drills such as "pump drill", "Feet together", "Step-through (Gary Player) drill", "Headcover outside the ball" and "Split-hands" were **not verified in this session**, so don't attribute them without a source.
- Coaches the brief named but not found with source-verified drills: Chris Como (only the chair drill), Mike Malaska, Shawn Clement, Rick Shiels, Me and My Golf, V1, Sportsbox AI.
- Not retrieved: the GOLF.com piece "5 drill mistakes that could be hurting your golf swing" (on how drills are misused). — [GOLF.com](https://golf.com/instruction/5-drill-mistakes-hurting-your-golf-swing)

## Q2. What are TPI's corrective exercises for the physical limitations behind each swing characteristic?

### Takeaway
TPI lists 16 "swing characteristics". For each one, TPI names the physical limitations behind it, the screen tests that reveal them, and named corrective exercises. The most common limitations it identifies are in the lead hip and the thoracic spine. The recurring causes are:
- restricted hip rotation or a limited deep squat/hip hinge;
- limited trunk–pelvis separation (thoracic spine mobility, lats);
- weak glutes/core (pelvic stabilizers).
TPI publishes exercise names, not written steps.

### Cited Findings
- **TPI's 16 swing characteristics:** S-Posture, C-Posture, Loss of Posture, Flat Shoulder Plane, Flying Elbow, Early Extension, Over the Top, Sway, Slide, Late Buckle, Reverse Spine Angle, Forward Lunge, Hanging Back, Casting, Scooping, Chicken Winging. — [TPI swing characteristics index](https://cdn.site.mytpi.com/improve-my-game/swing-characteristics)
- **Most common limitations:** "based on thousands of physical screens, the TPI identified the most common limitations occur in the 'Lead Hip' and 'Thoracic Spine'" (secondary, search summary). — [Golf Monthly](https://golfmonthly.com/features/my-golf-fitness-handicap-is-almost-20-shots-better-than-my-whs-index-the-game-changing-screening-all-golfers-should-try)
- **Early extension:**
  - **Limitations:** limited deep squat or full hip bend; restricted pelvic rotation around the lead hip; limited trunk-to-pelvis separation (spine/rib cage mobility, short lats); weak or poorly controlled pelvic stabilizers (glutes, abs).
  - **Exercises:** *Search and Destroy with Calf Stretch* (ankle dorsiflexion for the deep squat); *Disassociation Planks* (lower/upper separation); *Half-Kneeling Bounce Pass* (rotary power with a stable lower body).
  - Source: [TPI Early Extension](https://cdn.site.mytpi.com/improve-my-game/swing-characteristics/early-extension)
- **Over the top:**
  - **Limitations:** poor weight shift back → front; limited hip mobility; weak core and lower body (glutes, abs); difficulty separating lower from upper body.
  - **Exercises:** *Brettzel* (spiral-line/rotational mobility); *Stork Turns Supported* (separation); *Starfish Pattern 1* (hip mobility + core stability).
  - Source: [TPI Over the Top](https://cdn.site.mytpi.com/improve-my-game/swing-characteristics/over-the-top)
- **Sway:**
  - **Limitations:** restricted trail-hip internal rotation; limited trunk–pelvis separation (spine mobility, short lats); weak lateral stability of the trail leg (glute medius).
  - **Exercises:** *Hip Drops*; *Helicopter Turns*; *Resisted Half-Kneeling Lift, No Rotation to Rotation*; *Horizontal Chops, Wide to Narrow Base*; *Change of Direction*.
  - Source: [TPI Sway](https://cdn.site.mytpi.com/improve-my-game/swing-characteristics/sway)
- **Loss of posture** (limitation → TPI screen tests):
  - restricted deep squat/hip bend → *Overhead Deep Squat, Toe Touch*;
  - limited trunk–pelvis separation → *Seated Trunk Rotation, Reach/Roll/Lift, Lat Test*;
  - weak core (abs, glutes) → *Pelvic Tilt, Bridge w/ Leg Extension*;
  - poor hip/shoulder flexibility → *90/90, Lat Test, Lower Quarter Rotation*.
  - **Exercises:** *Two Arm Cross Body Lat Stretch*; *Windmills*; *Lunge Stance One Arm Incline Row*.
  - Source: [TPI Loss of Posture](https://cdn.site.mytpi.com/improve-my-game/swing-characteristics/loss-of-posture)
- **Not fixing everything [Consensus, secondary]:** physical limitations come from motor-control problems, joint stiffness or both. Some can't be fully fixed, so the plan may work around them. — [search summary of TPI-adjacent coaching sources, e.g. The Dan Plan on TPI](https://thedanplan.com/a-month-of-working-with-bruce-furman-and-some-titleist-tpi-thoughts/)
- **Debate on physical causes:** GOLF.com attributes early extension to physical limitations such as inflexible hips or a weak core. — [GOLF.com, is early extension good or bad](https://golf.com/instruction/is-early-extension-good-or-bad-heres-what-you-need-to-know/)

### Inferences
- When a swing analysis spots early extension, sway, loss of posture or OTT, a professional (TPI-informed) analysis adds a caveat: "this may be physical (hip rotation, thoracic mobility, glute/core strength); a TPI screen would confirm it". It does not assume the cause is purely technical. Recommending a TPI-certified screen is the safe, credible line.
- The pattern across TPI pages (hip mobility + T-spine/lat separation + glute/core stability) works as a general physical-cause checklist for the lower-body and posture characteristics.

### Gaps
- Not fetched: the TPI pages for Slide, Late Buckle, Reverse Spine Angle, Flying Elbow, Flat Shoulder Plane, Forward Lunge, Hanging Back, Casting, Scooping, Chicken Winging, S/C-Posture. Their URLs follow the pattern `cdn.site.mytpi.com/improve-my-game/swing-characteristics/<slug>`. Their exercises and limitations are **not documented here**.
- TPI exercise instructions are in video/members content. No written steps were found for any named exercise.
- The often-repeated claim that TPI's philosophy is "no one way to swing, but an efficient way for each body" was **not verified** in this session.

## Q3. How do PGA / TPI / top coaches prioritize faults and structure an analysis and lesson?

### Takeaway
Credible coaching sources agree on working backwards: **ball flight → impact factors (centeredness, face, path, attack angle, speed, dynamic loft) → swing/body cause → (body/physical cause) → one change at a time**. The coach treats a path or swing-position deviation as an *effect* until the *cause* (often pivot, setup or a physical limitation) is identified. Since the D-plane research, the face is understood to control start direction and the path to control curvature. Saying the reverse is an outdated "old ball flight law".

### Cited Findings
- **Diagnosis order:** "Ball flight > Impact factors > Swing principles (Swing changes)". Starting with swing changes can make ball flight worse (search summary; page 404'd on fetch). — [Golf Insider, How PGA Coaches Are Taught To Teach](https://golf-insider.beehiiv.com/p/how-pga-coaches-are-taught-to-teach)
- **[Opinion, PGA professional] Ryan Smith** (Lead Instructor, Marc Lapointe Golf Academy; Carolinas PGA, May 2020). Source: [Carolinas PGA, A practical application of swing mechanics for teaching and coaching](https://carolinas.pga.com/news/a-practical-application-of-swing-mechanics-for-teaching-and-coaching/)
  - Organizes every lesson around six laws of ball flight: centeredness of contact, face angle, clubhead path, clubhead speed, angle of approach, dynamic loft.
  - "All lessons I give have a primary objective": change ball flight through one of the six laws.
  - A method that doesn't change at least one law "inside of a singular golf lesson" may only alter swing aesthetics.
  - "The great coaches are experts in applying match ups, finding the missing link and changing ball flight."
  - The long-term goal is a golfer "capable of 'being their own coach.'"
- **Cause vs. effect:** a case where the EFFECT was an overly in-to-out path causing an inconsistent ball flight, but the CAUSE was a faulty pivot motion (search summary). — [GolfWRX, Stickney: fix the real cause of the problem with a launch monitor](https://golfwrx.com/237099/stickney-fix-the-real-cause-of-the-problem-with-a-launch-monitor/)
- **[Trackman/Hugh Marr] Outputs vs. inputs:** many commonly cited parameters (attack angle, club path) are not the primary inputs players control. Coaching should target the cause, the "true impact factors", not the combination factors (search summary). — [Trackman Talks: The True Impact Factors](https://www.trackman.com/blog/trackman-talks-the-true-impact-factors)
- **Read impact from ball flight:** players should be able to use ball flight to tell what happened at impact (Adam Young, per GolfWRX interview summary; indirect). — [GolfWRX](https://golfwrx.com/?p=442854)
- **Face and path:**
  - "Face sends it, path bends it": the D-plane shows the face controls start direction and the path controls curvature. — [TopSpeedGolf](https://members.topspeedgolf.com/?p=113015)
  - One blog's rough estimate is ~85% face / 15% path for start direction. Sources disagree on the split, so treat it as approximate. — [The DIY Golfer, Trackman for dummies](https://thediygolfer.com/trackman-golf-for-dummies)
  - The D-plane comes from Theodore Jorgensen's *The Physics of Golf* and challenged the prevailing view of initial direction. — [GolfWRX, Understanding the new ball flight laws](https://golfwrx.com/107406/understanding-the-new-ball-flight-laws/)
  - Many instructors still teach "starts on path, curves to face". Critics call this outdated. — [TheSandTrap, Ball Flight Laws](https://thesandtrap.com/b/playing_tips/ball_flight_laws)
- **Centeredness first:** a centered strike is needed for predictable curvature, because off-center hits invoke gear effect (search summary). — [GolfWRX](https://golfwrx.com/?p=107406)
- **[TPI] Body before technique:** TPI's Over the Top page advises starting with hip and spine mobility and then progressing to weight-shift sequencing drills. — [TPI OTT](https://cdn.site.mytpi.com/improve-my-game/swing-characteristics/over-the-top)
- **Ball flight → plane:** the curve reveals the path, which in turn hints at shaft-plane tendencies (search summary). — [Golf Channel/NBC Sports, make sure you know what your ball flight is telling you](https://www.nbcsports.com/golf/news/article-golf-instruction-make-sure-know-what-your-ball-flight-telling-you)
- **Positions are checkpoints, not causes:** P4 (top) and P7 (impact) are called the most critical checkpoints for face control and power. One critic argues "forces move the golf ball, not positions", treating positions as a teaching aid (search summary, exact page unverified). — [The DIY Golfer swing positions](https://thediygolfer.com/swing-positions); [Golf Insider UK](https://golfinsideruk.com/golf-swing-positions/)
- **P-system origin:** credited to Mac O'Grady (search summary of thediygolfer / Golf Insider UK). No link to Mike Adams was confirmed. — [The DIY Golfer](https://thediygolfer.com/swing-positions)
- **HackMotion's P-definitions** ([HackMotion, Golf Swing Positions](https://hackmotion.com/golf-swing-positions/)):

  | Position | Definition |
  |---|---|
  | P1 | Address |
  | P2 | Takeaway ending with the club parallel to the ground |
  | P3 | Lead arm parallel in the backswing |
  | P4 | Top |
  | P5 | "Transition from top until club is parallel on the way down" (ambiguous) |
  | P6 | Shaft parallel to the ground before impact |
  | P7 | Impact |
  | P8 | Release |
  | P9 | Lead arm parallel on the lead side, "mirror of P3" |
  | P10 | Finish |

  The same article's table and text disagree on P10. Sources define P5, P8 and P9 inconsistently.

### Inferences
- A professional-looking analysis is written in this order:
  1. what the ball does (or, without a launch monitor, what the video and ball flight suggest);
  2. what impact must look like to produce it (face/path/strike/low point);
  3. the root cause in the motion (pivot, setup, wrist condition at P4);
  4. a possible physical limitation (TPI);
  5. **one** priority change with one drill and its progression.
  Listing 8 faults with equal weight looks amateur. "One primary objective per lesson" (Smith) and "fix the cause, not the effect" (Stickney, Trackman) support this.
- Setup checks (grip, ball position, posture, alignment) usually come before in-swing fixes. Smith lists grip, ball position, pivot, distance from the ball and stance as the levers matched to the student. The explicit claim "fix the setup first" was **not found** as a quoted principle, so present it as common practice, not a cited rule.
- To avoid P-number errors, define the P-numbering used in the report explicitly (e.g. "P6 = shaft parallel in the downswing"), because sources differ on P5/P8/P9.

### Gaps
- Not retrieved: the official PGA of America curriculum text (e.g. PGA PGM "ball flight laws / cause-and-effect" model), Golf Digest's "Butch Harmon: 3 faults to fix + 3 to leave alone" (HTTP 403), and Adam Young's own text. — [Golf Digest, Butch Harmon](https://www.golfdigest.com/golf-instruction/2014-05/photos-butch-harmon-faults-to-fix) is worth retrieving by other means.
- No source fetched for TPI's formal "Body-Swing Connection" statement.

## Q4. What mistakes do online/AI swing analyses make that professionals criticize?

### Takeaway
The criticisms that hold up against sources are:
- calling debated or tour-normal patterns universal faults (early extension is the clearest example);
- using the old ball-flight laws (path = start direction) and treating path/attack angle as root causes;
- judging positions instead of impact and ball flight;
- treating a 2D camera's "plane" as real when camera height or offset distorts it;
- inconsistent P-numbering;
- mixing up TPI terms (sway vs. slide vs. late buckle vs. hanging back; casting vs. flip vs. scoop).

### Cited Findings
- **[Debate] Early extension isn't universally a fault:** some teachers cite Jack Nicklaus and argue it "may just be your unique way of creating power, based on your body type". GOLF Top 100 Teacher Terry Rowles is credited with the pro-extension argument, and Peter Kostis posted "So much for early extension being terrible for your swing!" — [GOLF.com, is early extension good or bad](https://golf.com/instruction/is-early-extension-good-or-bad-heres-what-you-need-to-know/)
- **Desirable extension:** "extension" has a good meaning too, the club extending away in the backswing for width. Mixing the two senses is a vocabulary error. — [Shark (Greg Norman), The Extension Bench](https://shark.com/golf-tips/the-extension-bench/)
- **Pelvis at impact in research data:** the pelvis and torso are both open at impact, the pelvis more (25–45°) than the torso (10–35°) (search summary). "Hips open at impact" is normal, not a fault. — [Penn State golf interactive, rotation](https://golf.psu.edu/interactives/rotation)
- **Old ball flight laws are outdated:** see Q3. — [TheSandTrap](https://thesandtrap.com/b/playing_tips/ball_flight_laws); [GolfWRX](https://golfwrx.com/107406/understanding-the-new-ball-flight-laws/)
- **Path/attack angle are outputs:** treating them as the cause is a symptom-level analysis. — [Trackman Talks](https://www.trackman.com/blog/trackman-talks-the-true-impact-factors)
- **[Vendor claim] Camera artifacts:** "If a camera is just two inches too high or off-center, the AI calculates a 'swing plane' that doesn't actually exist in 3D space." — [SwingMatch, why AI can't fix your golf swing](https://sm2-promo-git.pages.dev/blog/for-golfers/why-ai-cant-fix-your-golf-swing)
- **Hands-on app test:** after two months with six apps, a reviewer found AI useful for body angles, tempo and path direction, with quality varying "dramatically between apps". — [Fulcria Labs review](https://fulcria-labs.github.io/blog/ai-golf-swing-analyzer-review)
- **AI vs. human coach scores:** a research comparison reported AI feedback averaging 4.08 vs. human coaches' 4.42 (AI stronger on comprehensiveness) (search summary; paper not opened). — [AIRCC CS&IT paper](https://aircconline.com/csit/papers/vol15/csit152206.pdf)
- **Even vendor content contradicts itself:** HackMotion's OTT article calls a flat/slightly flexed lead wrist at the top both a problem and the goal. Copying drill content without checking produces contradictory advice. — [HackMotion OTT](https://hackmotion.com/drills-to-stop-coming-over-the-top/)
- **TPI's precise distinctions** ([TPI index](https://cdn.site.mytpi.com/improve-my-game/swing-characteristics)):

  | Characteristic | Phase | Direction / meaning |
  |---|---|---|
  | Sway | Backswing | Away from target |
  | Slide | Downswing | Toward target |
  | Late buckle | After impact | Slide-like |
  | Hanging back | Downswing | No forward shift |
  | Forward lunge | Transition | Upper-body lunge |
  | Early extension | Downswing | Lower body toward the ball |
  | Loss of posture | Whole swing | Any change of setup angles |
  | Flat shoulder plane | Backswing | Standing up |
  | Casting | Downswing | Early release |
  | Scooping | Impact | Lifting / adding loft |
  | Chicken winging | Through impact | Lead-arm breakdown |

- **Over the top must show in the club, not just the body:** TPI diagnoses OTT relative to a shaft-plane/lead-forearm "slot", down the line. — [TPI OTT](https://cdn.site.mytpi.com/improve-my-game/swing-characteristics/over-the-top)
- **Path vs. face in a slice:** OTT gives a pull with a square face and a slice with an open face (TPI). Calling every slice "over the top" ignores the face. — [TPI OTT](https://cdn.site.mytpi.com/improve-my-game/swing-characteristics/over-the-top)

### Inferences
- Red flags that make an analysis look amateur to a professional:
  - saying "the ball starts where the path goes";
  - calling every slice OTT without considering the face;
  - flagging hip thrust/standing up through impact or open hips at impact as faults on a good player;
  - judging "on plane" from a camera that isn't at hand height on the target line;
  - listing many faults with no priority;
  - using P-numbers without defining them;
  - mixing sway/slide, or calling casting a flip.
- Hedge debated items with "in your case, because X causes Y at impact". Reserve "fault" for patterns that demonstrably hurt impact (strike, face, low point) for this player.

### Gaps
- Not found: credible, named-coach published critiques of AI/online swing analyses. Sources found were vendor blogs or forums, apart from the GOLF.com early-extension debate.
- No source retrieved on which other patterns are tour-normal (e.g. lead-wrist bowing, trail-heel lift, head movement, across the line in some tour players). Verify these before claiming them.

## Q5. What Spanish terminology do Argentine / Spanish-speaking instructors use?

### Takeaway
Searches found **no authoritative Argentine or Spanish instructor glossary**: no results from AAG, RFEG, PGA de Argentina or Golf Digest Mexico/Argentina. The Spanish-language sources found show that English loanwords (backswing, downswing, slice, swing) are standard in Spanish golf writing, alongside a few Spanish terms ("plano del swing", "gancho", "colocación", "continuación/acabado"). Most Argentine-specific terms in the brief are still **unverified**.

### Cited Findings
- **"Plano del swing"** = swing plane; **"colocación"** = stance (vocabulary list; search summary). — [Wlingua, golf vocabulary in Spanish](https://learn.spanish.wlingua.com/golf-vocabulary-in-spanish/)
- **Loanwords in a Spanish-language article:** "backswing" and "downswing" are used as-is to describe the swing phases (search summary). — [Garmin España, ¿Cómo mejorar mi swing de golf?](https://www.garmin.com/es-ES/blog/como-mejorar-mi-swing-de-golf/)
- **Follow-through** is rendered as "continuación" or "acabado"; **"gancho"** = hook (curves sharply left for right-handers); **slice** = "slice" or "un corte" (bilingual glossary; search summary). — [Wlingua](https://learn.spanish.wlingua.com/golf-vocabulary-in-spanish/); [LanGeek word list](https://langeek.co/en-ES/vocab/subcategory/5856/word-list)
- Spanish golf media uses English technical terms with Spanish explanations (e.g. "Backdoor, Bite, Blade, Bounce y Break, son términos golfísticos"). — [OpenGolf.es](https://opengolf.es/?p=62468)

### Inferences
- A safe, professional style for an Argentine audience: Spanish words where they clearly exist ("plano", "impacto", "postura", "trayectoria", "cara del palo", "gancho"), and English loanwords where Spanish speakers already use them (backswing, downswing, slice, swing, "early extension"). Give the English term in parentheses the first time.

### Gaps (unverified; need an Argentine PGA professional or source)
